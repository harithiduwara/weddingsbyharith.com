About

I am the one you will forget is there

I have a BSc in Computer Science, University of Colombo, and I have never really used it.

That is not entirely fair. Four years of Computer Science taught me how to sit with a problem for a long time without panicking, and how to build things that keep working when nobody is watching them. Both of those turn out to be most of this job. The programming itself I gave up somewhere around 2017, which is when I started photographing weddings and stopped doing anything else.

That was 9 years ago. I still get a slightly sick feeling in the car on the way to a ceremony, which I have decided is a good sign.

Most of what I know I learned assisting and second-shooting for photographers a great deal better than I was, carrying their bags and being told where not to stand. It is the only real apprenticeship this trade has, and I would not trust anybody who skipped it.

How I actually work

Documentary is not a filter you apply in Lightroom. It is a set of decisions you make on the day: use the light that is in the room rather than the light you brought, keep working while everyone eats, and trust that what is happening in front of you is more interesting than anything you could arrange.

In practice that means I turn up early and I am quiet. I will have walked your venue. I will know roughly what time the sun clears the wall and which corner of the hall goes dark first. You should never have to think about any of it.

Family groups are the exception and I do them gladly. Quickly, in one place, from a list we agree beforehand. Fifteen minutes, not an hour. You will want them in twenty years even if you resent them on the day.

I work all over the island and I will travel further if you are getting married somewhere else. I have photographed poruwa ceremonies before six in the morning, churches in the rain, and one wedding where the generator failed during the speeches and everybody carried on by phone torch. That one was excellent.
