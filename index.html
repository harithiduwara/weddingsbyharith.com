The short version

You will barely notice me. You will absolutely notice the photographs.

I photograph weddings the way a documentary is filmed: I turn up early, work out where the light and the people are going to be, and then get out of the way.

There is no shot list to grind through and no hour spent arranging strangers in front of a hedge. What you get back is the actual texture of the day — your uncle mid-anecdote, the ten seconds before the doors opened, your mother's hands.

How it goes

Three conversations and one very long day

We talk first

A call with no pitch in it. We work out what your day looks like, what you actually want photographed, and whether we get on — which matters more than any portfolio, given I will be next to you for ten hours.

I do the homework

I walk your venue, find where the light lands at six, and build a rough timeline with you. By the morning of the wedding there is nothing left to decide.

Then I disappear

On the day I am quiet, early, and mostly behind you. Family groups take fifteen minutes because we planned them. The rest is yours.
