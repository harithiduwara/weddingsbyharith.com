The short version

You will barely notice me. You will notice the photographs.

I photograph weddings the way a documentary gets filmed. I turn up early, work out where the light and the people are going to be, and then get out of the way.

There is no shot list to grind through and no hour spent arranging relatives in front of a hedge. What comes back is the actual texture of the day. Your uncle mid-anecdote. The ninety seconds before the doors opened. Your mother’s hands.

Weddings since 2019, paid work since 2016, and somewhere past a thousand shoots. I have still never photographed two weddings that were the same shape.

How it goes

Three conversations and one very long day

We talk first

A call with no pitch in it. What your day actually looks like, what you want photographed, and whether we get on. That last one matters more than any portfolio, given I will be standing next to you for ten hours.

I do the homework

I visit your venue, find where the light lands at five, and build a rough running order with you around your nekath. By the morning of the wedding there is nothing left to decide.

Then I disappear

On the day there are two or three of us, early and quiet and mostly behind you. Family groups take fifteen minutes because we planned them properly. The rest of it is yours.
